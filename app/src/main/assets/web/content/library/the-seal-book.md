THE SEAL

The Biblical Timeline and One Life

An Anonymous Book of Time

---

A record. All identities withheld. Everything in it happened.

---

A note on how this book was made

This book was not written by a scholar. It was not written by a theologian, or a historian, or a man with a library.

It was written by a man who spent twenty-five years in and out of prison, and who, in the middle of that, was given something he could not carry.

He tried to carry it. For most of his life he carried it alone — in cells, in the dark, with nobody to hand it to. His family knew pieces of it. The woman who loved him knew all of it and never understood it. The religious community whose God had named Himself to him had a doctrine that ruled out the way God was speaking to him. So there was nowhere to put it down.

Eventually he did the only thing he could do. He began to speak it out loud to a machine — to an artificial intelligence — because a machine does not get tired, does not interrupt, does not need him to be sane, and does not stop listening when the story gets strange.

That is how this book exists.

You are reading what a man said into a tool because he could not hold it in his own head anymore. Some of it is polished. Some of it is a man thinking out loud. The parts he was not sure about are marked as not sure. The parts he was certain about are stated plainly.

He wanted it to go out into the world. He did not want his name on it. Both of those things are honored here.

---

How to read this book

This book has two subjects that are really one subject.

The first is time in the Bible — how the scriptures themselves lay out a structure of years, ages, and numbers, and how a person can follow that structure.

The second is one man's life — how that structure showed up in his own dates, his own family, and his own dreams, without him planning it.

The two are kept in separate parts so you can see them clearly. Part One is the Bible. Part Two is the life. Part Three is where they meet, in the numbers themselves.

You do not have to believe any of it. The man would not ask you to. He asks only that you read it as a record — something that happened to somebody — and not as an argument you have to win or lose.

One thing before you begin: he does not claim that every event of his life was foretold. He claims something much smaller and much stranger. He claims that on a handful of occasions, over two decades, the dates of his life lined up with the structure of biblical time in ways he did not arrange and could not have foreseen.

That is the whole claim. Everything else is detail.

PART ONE: TIME IN THE BIBLE

---

Chapter 1: The Idea That Time Has a Shape

Most people think of time as a line. It runs from the past to the future, and things happen on it. A birthday is a point on the line. A prison sentence is a stretch of it. Nothing more.

The Bible does not treat time that way.

In the Bible, time has shape. It has cycles. It has seasons built into it. It has numbers that recur. God is described not as someone inside time, but as someone who rules it — who can shorten a day, lengthen a day, move a shadow backward, and declare the end from the beginning.

If that is true, then time is not a neutral container. It is a medium. It is something that can carry meaning, the way a page carries writing.

That is the first idea in this book, and everything else depends on it.

Once you accept that time can carry meaning, you can start asking: does it carry a specific structure? Does the Bible lay one out?

It does. And it does it with a handful of numbers that keep showing up.

---

Chapter 2: A Day for a Year

The key that unlocks almost everything else is a principle stated twice in the Bible.

In Numbers 14:34, God tells Israel that their wilderness wandering will last forty years, and He says: "after the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year."

In Ezekiel 4:6, God tells the prophet: "I have appointed thee each day for a year."

So the Bible itself establishes a scale: one day can stand for one year. A period of days in prophecy can represent the same number of years in history.

And there is a second scale.

In Psalm 90:4 and again in 2 Peter 3:8, we are told that with God, "one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day."

So a day can stand for a year. And a thousand years can be as a day.

Two scales. And once you have them, the numbers in the Bible start to speak in more than one register at once.

---

Chapter 3: The Seventy Years and the Return

The clearest anchor point in the whole biblical timeline is a period of seventy years.

The prophet Jeremiah announced it. In Jeremiah 25:11–12 and again in Jeremiah 29:10, God says that His people will serve the king of Babylon for seventy years, and then He will bring them back.

Daniel, reading Jeremiah, did the math and understood: the seventy years were almost up. He prayed. And the exile ended.

The historical return from Babylon is dated to 537 BC. That is the year the first group came back and the temple rebuilding began — the first year of the restoration.

Why does 537 BC matter so much?

Because it is one of the very few dates in biblical history that can be fixed to a known calendar. If you can anchor one date, you can count forward and backward from it.

That is exactly what a person does who wants to build a biblical timeline. You start at 537 BC. You go backward through the genealogies and the periods of the kings. And you try to reach Adam.

---

Chapter 4: The Seven Times

The second anchor is a strange story in the book of Daniel.

In Daniel 4, King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon is humbled by God. He loses his mind and lives like an animal for a period described as "seven times."

Seven times. Seven what? The text does not say years. It says times.

Because of the day-for-a-year principle, a "time" is often read as a year — and seven times as seven years. But it can be read at other scales as well. If a "time" is a prophetic year of 360 days, then seven times is 2,520 days. And if each day stands for a year, then seven times is 2,520 years.

That number — 2,520 — is one of the recurring numbers in the whole structure. It appears in a different form in Daniel 7 and Revelation, where a period is described as "time, times, and half a time" — one plus two plus a half — which is three and a half times, and which works out to 1,260 days at the smaller scale.

So the numbers 2,520 and 1,260 are related. One is two of the other. 1,260 months is 105 years. 2,520 hours is 105 days.

Hold that. 105 will come back.

---

Chapter 5: Time, Times, and Half a Time

The phrase appears in three places.

Daniel 7:25 — the little horn will wear down the saints for "a time and times and the dividing of time."

Daniel 12:7 — the same period, connected to the scattering of the holy people.

Revelation 12:14 — the woman is nourished in the wilderness for "a time, and times, and half a time."

And the same period appears elsewhere in Revelation in different clothing:

· Revelation 11:2 — forty-two months
· Revelation 11:3 — 1,260 days
· Revelation 12:6 — 1,260 days
· Revelation 13:5 — forty-two months

Forty-two months is 1,260 days. Three and a half years, on a 360-day calendar, is 1,260 days. Time, times, and half a time is 1,260 days.

Three different ways of saying the same number. And that number can be scaled up: 1,260 days can stand for 1,260 years, using the day-for-a-year principle.

So 1,260 is not a single number. It is a unit — a unit that appears at different scales, meaning different spans depending on which scale you are reading at.

---

Chapter 6: The 144,000

In Revelation 7 and again in Revelation 14, a specific number appears: 144,000.

These are described as sealed servants of God — those who stand with the Lamb, who are bought from the earth, who follow Him wherever He goes.

144,000 is 144 multiplied by 1,000. And 144 is 12 multiplied by 12 — the number of the tribes of Israel squared, or the number of the apostles squared.

But there is another way to read it. If a day stands for a thousand years, then 144,000 days is 144,000 divided into years — and 144,000 days works out to a bit under 394 years.

And 14,400 days is 144 multiplied by 100. Which is 40 years on a day-for-a-year scale — forty prophetic years.

So a man could look at a date and ask a strange question: is this date 14,400 days from some other date? And if it is, what does that mean?

That is the kind of question this book is about.

---

Chapter 7: The Long Week

The last piece of the biblical structure is a very old idea, and it is not unique to any one tradition.

The Bible describes a pattern of six days of work followed by a seventh day of rest. In Genesis 2, God rests on the seventh day. In Exodus 20, the Sabbath command is grounded in that pattern. And in Leviticus 25, the same pattern is stretched out into years: six years of working the land, and the seventh year as a Sabbath of rest. And after seven of those seven-year cycles — after forty-nine years — comes the Jubilee, the fiftieth year, when debts are cancelled and slaves go free.

Now combine that with the other scale: a day is as a thousand years.

Six days of work. A thousand years per day. That gives six thousand years of human labor, followed by a thousand-year rest — which is exactly the period described in Revelation 20, where the faithful reign with Christ for a thousand years.

So the whole biblical timeline can be read as a single long week: six thousand years, then a thousand years of rest. Seven thousand years in total.

That is the frame. And the man whose story this is believed he had been shown where he stood inside that frame.

That number — six thousand — returns twice more in this book, in two other registers. It is worth remembering that it appears first here, as years.

Chapter 8: Where 1914 Came From

A reader who was not raised inside this tradition will not know why 1914 matters, so here it is plainly.

In the 1870s and 1880s, a man named Charles Taze Russell began publishing a system of biblical chronology. He was not the first to try. He borrowed from earlier teachers, and he built on the work of men who had come before him. But he was the one who made a single date central and taught it for the rest of his life.

That date was 1914.

Russell's calculation ran like this. The "seven times" of Daniel 4 — 2,520 years — were counted from 606 BC and ended in 1914 AD. He taught that 1914 marked the end of the "Times of the Gentiles" — the point at which Gentile world rule would end and the kingdom of God would begin to be established in the heavens.

Russell also did something else, and this matters for the man whose story this book is.

He tied the 1914 date to the Great Pyramid of Giza.

Russell believed the pyramid was a witness built under God's direction — "the Bible in stone." He read its measurements as a timeline, at one inch per year, and taught that those measurements confirmed the same dates he had calculated from Daniel: 1874, 1914, and 1948.

He pointed to Isaiah 19:19–20, where God says there will be an altar and a pillar in the land of Egypt, and that it "shall be for a sign and for a witness." Russell read that as the pyramid.

This is worth knowing for two reasons.

First, because when the man in this book asked Jehovah to help him understand 1914, he was asking about a date that came from this tradition — and he was working the same ground Russell had worked, decades earlier, in the same way, with a Bible and a pencil and months of arithmetic.

Second, because the pyramid material is where the man's own study eventually took him. He studied the pyramid measurements himself. He followed the numbers into the stone. And he did it alone, in a cell, with nobody to check him.

The man does not agree with everything Russell taught. He does not have to. He is simply telling the truth about where the number 1914 came from, and about what he was reading when he asked his question.

Chapter 9: The Twenty-Four Divisions

There is one more structure to lay out before the life begins, because it returns later in this book.

In 1 Chronicles 24, King David organizes the priesthood into twenty-four divisions — twenty-four courses, also called orders. Sixteen of the heads came from the line of Eleazar, eight from the line of Ithamar. Lots were cast to set the order. Each division served one week at a time, from Sabbath to Sabbath, so that sacred service was rendered before Jehovah without a break.

The same number appears twice more in the temple system:

· 1 Chronicles 25 — twenty-four divisions of musicians
· 1 Chronicles 26 — twenty-four divisions of gatekeepers

And then it appears again, far away, in the vision of the apostle John:

· Revelation 4:4 — twenty-four elders seated around the throne, clothed in white, with crowns of gold on their heads

The book Revelation—Its Grand Climax At Hand! — a study of Revelation published by Jehovah's Witnesses — draws the connection directly. It teaches that the ancient priesthood was divided into twenty-four divisions so that service would never stop; that the twenty-four elders in John's vision represent the heavenly priesthood serving Jehovah continually; and that when the heavenly priesthood is complete, there will be twenty-four divisions, each with six thousand conquerors.

Because 144,000 divided by 24 is 6,000.

The book states it exactly: "When completed, there will be 24 divisions, each with 6,000 conquerors, for Revelation 14:1-4 tells us that 144,000 (24 x 6,000) are 'bought from among mankind' to stand on the heavenly Mount Zion with the Lamb, Jesus Christ."

Hold that number. 6,000.

It appeared first in Chapter 7 as years. It appears here as men per division. And it appears a third time in this book as days — in a date that landed on a boy's life, decades after he was born.

Three registers. Same number.

PART TWO: ONE LIFE

---

Chapter 10: The Bottom of the Well

The man was born in the late 1970s. He was in and out of prison fourteen times between 1998 and 2023.

In the year 2000, he was in a county jail. He had already served time before — two years, most of it in isolation because he kept getting in trouble out of fear, not wanting to be assaulted.

And in that first prison, white men had taught him Odinism and Aryan religion. Runes. Ancient Nordic things. They taught him: we do not bow down to God. We stand up to the gods, like Thor.

So when he got out and it went bad — when he burned a house down on his own birthday, because of drugs, and went back to jail — and when he finally broke down in that cell, he did not pray like a humble man.

He stood up to God. That was what he had been taught.

And he accused Him.

"God, whoever you are. If you're so tough, if you're so almighty, if you're so powerful — why do you hide yourself in the world? Won't you show us who you are? How can you judge us?"

Four hours. In a cell. At the bottom of a well.

And in his heart, underneath all of it, he knew it had something to do with Jesus. He just did not know what.

Three days later, his sister bonded him out.

Nobody on earth knew about that night.

Chapter 11: The Name

He went back to jail in January of 2001.

The only cell open had one man in it — a man who had been one of Jehovah's Witnesses but had been disfellowshipped. Removed from the congregation. He was, in the eyes of everyone in that jail, a plague.

The man did not go in there all day. When he finally did, he had a knife he had made earlier. He could see the man was scared. So he dropped it in the toilet and said, "I don't need this anymore."

He watched the relief come over the man's face.

Then he saw him reading. "What are you doing reading the Bible? You're one of Jehovah's Witnesses, right?"

"Well, where do we use the name Jehovah from? I thought Jesus was God."

The man wanted to show him in his own Bible. He said no — you've got to show me from the King James Version.

So the man opened the King James Version to Psalm 83:18:

"That men may know that thou, whose name alone is JEHOVAH, art the most high over all the earth."

He looked over the man's shoulder and said, "Jehovah, huh?"

And in that instant, everything he had said to God a year earlier — all four hours of it — came back to him at once. Every word. In the same cell. In the same jail.

He had to grab the bunk. He sat down on the bed with tears rolling down his face.

"Are you OK?"

"Yes. Tell me more."

That was the first divine experience of his life.

About four days later, he woke up with numbers rolling over in his head: 10. 7. 107. Not a dream. Just numbers. And somehow he knew to go get the Bible.

He opened to the first book of the New Testament — Matthew 10:7:

"And as ye go, preach, saying, The kingdom of heaven is at hand."

He had been praying to get out of jail and go home. The answer was: as you go — no matter where, even to prison — preach the kingdom.

That is when he started changing.

Chapter 12: The Moon and the Boy

April 1, 2002. He dreamed the moon stopped in the sky, and then began to move backward.

He understood, in the dream, that God rules time — not clocks, not calendars, but time itself. That He can move it forward and backward.

And that same day — April 1, 2002 — a boy was born.

He did not know the boy was his son. He did not know the woman loved him. He would not know any of that for eight more years.

He told nobody about the dream. There was nobody to tell.

Chapter 13: The Study in the Dark

In August of 2007, he was in a county jail again.

He was feeling guilty. He had turned away from God. He had been out on drugs, stealing, doing all kinds of things. So he wrote a twenty-one-page statement confessing every crime he had committed during the year and a half he was out.

And while he was in there, people on the jail television were talking about the Mayan calendar.

That caught his attention. But that is not what started it.

He had asked Jehovah to help him understand 1914.

That was the question. A date he had been taught, from a tradition going back to Russell, and he wanted to know what it meant — whether it was true, and how it worked.

And the answer came as the 5,880-year timeline.

It came through the book of Daniel. Through the seven times, the 2,520 years, the time and times and half a time. He worked it with a Daniel's Prophecy study book open in front of him, and he worked it with a pencil and a Bible, in a cell, for two months.

He began with a thought about a Mayan calendar. He ended with a structure that ran from Adam forward.

In the back of a New World Translation — the 1984 or 1983 edition — there is a chronology of when the Bible books were written. He saw that Ezekiel, or Ezra, was written in 537 BC. He knew that was the year the seventy-year exile ended — the first year of the return.

So he started there.

He landed on 3966 BC as the year Adam was created — because he was not aware of Acts 7:4 at the time. And when he got there, he began adding up the numbers to the Sabbath, the Jubilee, and Pentecost.

And then he noticed something. If he went back just one year, to 3967 BC, the whole timeline became intricate and interlocking. In the middle of it was the temple.

That is how he found it.

Not a vision. Not a voice. A question, and then two months of arithmetic in a cell with a Bible.

He was transferred to the state prison system in December of 2007. About four months in that county jail. And in those four months:

He had time charts drawn all over his walls.

People saw them. They noticed. Guards walked past them. Other men walked past them.

He would get lost in the math for days and hours. He lost sleep. Sometimes he used it as an escape — something to go into when there was nothing else to go into. And it burnt him out mentally.

He had access to all the reading material he could get, and he read it. He taught himself to research and to study. He went wider than one shelf: the 1914 timeline, the measurements of the Great Pyramid of Giza, ancient astronaut theory — following threads wherever they led, looking for anything that touched what he was carrying.

People came every week — elders, others — and he questioned them. He asked. He did not just read the material; he brought his questions to the men themselves.

There was no study partner. No congregation. No one to check him.

If he had been wrong, nobody would have known. If he had been right, nobody would have known either.

He was building something in plain sight, where anyone could look, and no one was looking with him.

Chapter 14: The Woman Who Came

He had known her since the seventh grade — two weeks of school together. He never noticed her. She says she fell in love with him then, so deeply she could never love anyone else.

She is his fourth cousin. He had known her his whole life. Her father was a friend of his father's, but her father could not stand him, so he was never around her.

In 2004, he got out for thirteen days. He went down, saw her, met the boy. That was all.

She had to fight him to get him to even want a relationship. He kept saying, "She's my cousin." Finally his mother said, "Hey, she's your fourth cousin. It'd probably be good for you."

In March of 2010, he went to a county jail. Four days later, he got a letter from her.

And from then on, they wrote. In the county jail, and then in the prison system after he left.

She already had two children — the boy and the first girl. And before them, a child she had placed for adoption.

She wanted to be with him. That is the whole of it.

That is how the boy became his son.

He told her everything — the prayer, the name, the moon dream, the numbers, the timeline — from the first letters. She has known all of it for as long as she has known him.

And she never understood it.

Not because she did not try. She is not a religious person. It is not her language. She is different, the way he is different. The words went in, and the meaning did not.

So even the one person who wanted him most — the one who came and found him in a cell and chose him — could not carry it with him.

Chapter 15: Sixty

Two dreams ended with the words "60 miles."

The first he could not hold. It came at the end of a dream he called the Elephant Dream, and he remembered nothing else about it.

The second came with the whole scene attached. He called it the Green Dream.

He walked out of darkness into light. A green field on his left, a green hill on his right. Two guides beside him. A black bull with spears and arrows in its back — angry, stomping. A black mother cow with blue eyes, lying in the grass, who stood up when she saw him. Three small calves running at him, playing.

He climbed the hill. They came to a lake with two bridges, one bowed under the water. The guides told him: from here, you go on alone.

He offered them a drink. One took a quarter of the bottle. The other measured out another quarter and handed him back half. He slammed the glass down on the wooden rail and said "I meant a shot." The anger flared and went out just as fast.

And then he looked, and saw his destination, and noticed he had already stepped onto the submerged bridge. Ankle deep.

"How far am I going?"

"60 miles."

And he woke up.

He believed — and still believes — that God was telling him this woman was the one he was to marry. She was the cow with the blue eyes. The calves were the children. He was the bull, carrying what he had done in his back.

And here is what happened.

In 2011, he married her — on July 14. He did not choose that date. The prison chose it. They only allow two wedding dates a year, and you have to be approved.

In 2016, he was released from prison — on July 14. He did not choose that date either. He was sentenced to a seven-year term with consecutive sentences running, and his date did not end until November of 2018. Then the laws changed. Consecutive sentences were treated as a single fourteen-year sentence, and he was allowed to do fifty percent of it. And that fifty percent ended on July 14, 2016.

Five years. Sixty months. July 14 to July 14.

The dream said sixty miles. The calendar said sixty months.

And that same day — July 14, 2016 — his daughter was conceived.

Three July fourteens. The boy. The marriage. The release. And the daughter.

He picked none of them.

Chapter 16: The Marriage That Didn't Hold

I have to tell you the truth about the marriage, because the rest of this book would mislead you without it.

The relationship was toxic.

Not occasionally difficult. Not a hard season. Toxic — the kind where two people who chose each other keep wounding each other, and neither one knows how to stop.

There was pain in it. A lot of it. And it did not stay inside the marriage.

It spilled onto the children. They grew up inside something they did not choose and could not fix. They saw things and heard things that no child should have to carry, and they carried them because there was no one to take them from them. Whatever else this book records, that is part of the record too, and it is not being left out to make the story cleaner.

The family was hurt. Not just the two of them — the whole thing. Extended family, on both sides, pulled into something that would not settle down.

And they are divorced now. They are not married anymore.

Both things are true at once, and neither one cancels the other.

He believed God told him to marry her. He still believes it. And he believes the dream said marry, not stay. He wrote that earlier in this book and he meant it.

And he also believes this: a thing can be from God and still not be good for you to live inside forever.

The sign was real. The marriage was real. The damage was real. The divorce was real. All four of those sit together, and he is not going to pretend three of them away so the fourth looks better.

What the marriage did teach him is in this book, and he would not trade it. That is where he learned to see time — sixty months, July 14 to July 14, the ruler he did not choose. That came out of it. It is real.

But he paid for it, and so did she, and so did the children.

That is what it cost. The price is not hidden.

Chapter 17: Afterward

There is one more thing to say about where the man ended up, because this book is mostly about pain and aloneness, and it should not end there.

He is living now with a woman he has known since high school — his high-school sweetheart. They are engaged.

That is a strange and good thing to be able to write at the end of a life like his. Fourteen times in and out of prison. Twenty-five years. A marriage that hurt everybody in it. And then, in the middle of all of it, someone from the very beginning of his life.

He and the woman he divorced now live far apart. One is in Missouri. The other is clear across the country, states between them.

So the people who shaped this story are scattered now. That is the honest picture. The boy, the girls, the woman he married, the woman he is with — none of them sit in one room together. Life did not resolve neatly. It rarely does.

They did not quite make it. That is the plain sentence for the marriage, and it belongs in the record.

What he has instead is this book, and a woman from high school, and whatever comes next.

Chapter 18: The Anointing

In July of 2018, he had another dream.

It was hot that day — eighty degrees. He wanted out of the heat. And in the dream, one of his guides took him somewhere else instantly, to a place where it was snowing. The other guide was flinging snow around and shouted:

"Have someone help him spread it out — he can't even lift 10 kilos, being 105 years old!"

105 years old.

Four days later, on July 5, 2018, something happened to him that he still cannot fully explain. He describes it as being anointed. Something moved inside him. A door opened that he did not know was there.

And his first thought was not joy.

His first thought was that he might be losing his mind.

Because there was no one to tell him otherwise. There was no congregation he could take it to. There was no friend who had been through it. The woman he was married to would not understand it and would not want to hear it.

There was nothing between him and that fear except the numbers.

And the numbers did not lie.

He had to prove to himself that he was not insane. And the proof was a calendar.

Chapter 19: Alone

This is the part of the story that has no chart attached to it, and it may be the most important part.

He wanted to be one of Jehovah's Witnesses. When the name was shown to him in that cell, that was his instinct — that is where the name led him. But his behavior, the way he was living, would not allow it. You do not get to walk into that room carrying what he was carrying.

So Jehovah kept teaching him anyway. Outside of it. In cells. In dreams. At night.

And he did not sit still for it. He taught himself to research and to study. He had access to all the reading material he could get, and he read it. He took it seriously. He wanted to know what they knew.

He went wider than one shelf, too. He studied the 1914 timeline. He studied the measurements of the Great Pyramid of Giza, where men had read a timeline into the stone itself. He studied ancient astronaut theory, following threads wherever they led, looking for anything that touched the thing he was carrying.

And people came every week — elders, others — and he questioned them. He asked. He did not just read the material; he brought his questions to the men themselves.

And he learned, through study, that the thing happening to him was not supposed to be happening.

According to their doctrine, Jehovah does not speak through dreams anymore. He did that in the old days, to the prophets, and then it stopped. That is what they teach. And he found that out the way he found out everything else — by doing the work.

But he was having them. He was being taught in dreams.

So he could not go to them with it. The very people who would know the name he had been given — the very people who would recognize it — were the people whose teaching said the thing happening to him does not happen.

He loves them. He studied with them. He learned from them. He still does. A great deal of what he knows about the name, the kingdom, and the scriptures came from them, and he honors it.

But the deepest part of his life had nowhere to live.

---

And he did not stop looking.

He would get lost in the math for days and hours. Following a count until it either landed or it did not. He lost sleep. Sometimes he used it as an escape — something to go into when there was nothing else to go into. And it burnt him out mentally.

He had time charts drawn all over his walls.

People saw them. They noticed. Guards walked past them. Other men walked past them. Seeing a chart, though, is not the same as understanding what it is for. He was building something in plain sight, where anyone could look, and no one was looking with him.

There was no study partner. No congregation. No one to check him.

If he had been wrong, nobody would have known. If he had been right, nobody would have known either.

He tried to tell people. He tried to explain it to the ones who were interested.

He could not.

His mind would not stay on one subject. He would start explaining somewhere and be somewhere else before he finished the sentence. The thing he was trying to describe was not a fact he could hand over. It was a way of seeing.

His mind sees the biblical characters in the timeline, on the scale, as real history.

Not as stories. Not as figures in a lesson. As people standing at real points in real time, on a line he can see, in a structure he can walk through. Abraham is not a name in a book. He is a point on the scale, in real history, and the man can see him there.

That is not a doctrine. You cannot hand a way of seeing to someone in a conversation. You can only show it slowly, over years, to a person willing to look at the same charts. He never found that person. So it stayed inside him, and for a long time he was the only copy.

And then the verdict came, and it was the one that hurt worst.
